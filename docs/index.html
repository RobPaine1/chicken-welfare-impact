Step 2. How much did that chicken suffer?

The Welfare Footprint Project estimated how many hours an average conventional meat chicken spends in pain over its life of about 47 days. Your meal carries the fraction of those hours found in step 1:

By cause:

The same hours split by how bad the pain is, rather than what caused it. Here the project publishes a 90% range for each estimate, shown in brackets. Read it as: nine times out of ten the true value is expected to fall inside the bracket.

Most of the uncertainty is in these pain estimates, not in the weights: the ranges are roughly ±40% around the central value. The by-cause breakdown has no published ranges but rests on the same evidence, so treat it as carrying about the same uncertainty. The two breakdowns also come from different releases of the same data and differ by about 4%, so read the by-cause numbers as proportions rather than exact hours. The bracket on the total simply adds the individual ranges, which slightly overstates its width.

  8. Pain intensity definitions: Welfare Footprint Institute.

Not included: pain during transport and slaughter, breeder birds, and diseases the project did not model. The numbers are conservative.
