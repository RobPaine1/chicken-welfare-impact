How this is calculated

Every number on this page comes from one chain of multiplications. There is no model and no fitting, just published averages strung together. Here it is in words:

animals used = cooked meat eaten ÷ cooking yield ÷ raw edible meat per animal days of life = animals used × days each animal lives hours of pain = animals used × hours of pain per animal (chicken only)

What this leaves out

  • Only chicken has pain estimates. Nobody has yet published comparable cumulative-pain figures for pigs, cattle or turkeys, so those meats show animals and days of life only. That is not a claim that they suffer less.
  • Pain during transport, stunning and slaughter is excluded. The Welfare Footprint Project has separate estimates for that stage.
  • Breeder birds (the parents of meat chickens, who are kept chronically hungry) are excluded. Their pain would be spread across the roughly 150 chicks each hen produces.
  • Diseases and injuries WFP did not model (contact dermatitis, infections, muscle disorders) are excluded, so the pain figures are conservative.
  • Averages hide variation. Slower-growing or higher-welfare chicken cuts the Disabling pain by about two-thirds. Cull dairy cows and calves are folded into the beef average.
  • Supply response. Eating one less chicken does not reduce production by exactly one chicken; economists estimate the long-run effect at roughly 0.3–0.8 animals per animal not eaten. This page reports the direct footprint without that adjustment.
