Step 1. How much of a chicken is that?

1a. Undo the cooking

Cooked meat weighs about three quarters of what it did raw.

  • Wraw = weight of the raw meat
  • Wcooked = weight of the cooked chicken you ate
  • y = cooking yield, the share of raw weight left after cooking

1b. Raw meat from one chicken

U.S. averages for 2024.

  • M = raw edible meat from one chicken
  • P = total live weight of all meat chickens produced in a year
  • N = number of meat chickens produced in that year (so P/N is one bird's live weight)
  • d = dressing: the carcass as a share of live weight
  • e = edible share: boneless meat as a share of the carcass

These are industry averages. Individual birds and cuts vary by roughly ±10%, and no formal interval is published for them.

1c. Fraction of a chicken

  • f = fraction of one chicken's meat that was on your plate

Step 2. How much did that chicken suffer?

The Welfare Footprint Project estimated how many hours an average conventional meat chicken spends in pain over its life of about 47 days. They ran the estimate many times with the inputs varied over their plausible ranges and report the mean and a 90% interval (the 5th to 95th percentile of those runs). They do not publish a median or a 95% interval. Your meal carries the fraction f of those hours:

  • hi = hours of pain from cause (or intensity) i behind your meal
  • Hi = mean hours of pain from cause i over one chicken's life
  • Hilo, Hihi = the 5th and 95th percentiles of that estimate; multiplying by f carries the interval through

2a. By cause

Mean hours per chicken from the project's per-harm dataset. The project publishes bounds for each cause as well; they are not yet included here.

2b. By intensity

The same hours split by how bad the pain is, rather than what caused it. Mean, then the 90% interval in brackets: nine times out of ten the true value is expected to fall inside it.

Most of the uncertainty is in these pain estimates, not in the weights: the intervals are roughly ±40% around the mean. The by-cause breakdown rests on the same evidence, so treat it as carrying about the same uncertainty. The two breakdowns come from different releases of the same data and differ by about 4%, so read the by-cause numbers as proportions rather than exact hours. The bracket on the total simply adds the individual intervals, which slightly overstates its width.

Sources

  1. Live weight (P, N): USDA NASS, Poultry Production and Value 2024 Summary (9.33 billion broilers, 61.1 billion lb).
  2. Dressing d = 0.75: University of Maine Extension, Understanding Poultry Yields.
  3. Edible share e = 0.74: University of Wisconsin Extension, Bird Breakdown.
  4. Cooking yield y = 0.75: USDA Table of Cooking Yields for Meat and Poultry.
  5. Life of 47 days: National Chicken Council, U.S. Broiler Performance.
  6. Hours of pain by intensity, mean and 90% interval: Welfare Footprint Institute, Broilers, conventional scenario. Consistent with their finding that the Better Chicken Commitment prevents 33 [13 to 53] h of Disabling, 79 [-99 to 260] h of Hurtful and 25 [5 to 45] s of Excruciating pain per bird.
  7. Hours of pain by cause: Welfare Footprint Institute, Pain-Track for broilers, "time in pain per harm" for the average bird.
  8. Pain intensity definitions: Welfare Footprint Institute.

Not included: pain during transport and slaughter, breeder birds, and diseases the project did not model. The numbers are conservative.
