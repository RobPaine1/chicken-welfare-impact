Hours of pain per chicken lifetime (H)

The Welfare Footprint Project estimated how many hours an average conventional meat chicken spends in pain over its life. For each cause they combined how many birds are affected, how long it lasts, and how intense it is, then summed over the bird's life. That is why lifetime does not appear as a separate multiplier: it is the window over which the hours accumulate.

Over a lifetime T of 47 days that is hours, so the average bird is in some level of pain for about of its life.

The causes (Hi, mean hours per lifetime)
  • Lameness: leg and joint disorders from growing too fast; walking hurts or becomes impossible.
  • No foraging or exploring: barren sheds with nothing to scratch, peck or investigate.
  • No dustbathing: no loose, dry substrate for the bathing behaviour that keeps feathers healthy.
  • No perching: no raised places to rest, which chickens seek out for safety.
  • Heat stress: heavy birds in crowded sheds cannot shed heat; they pant and become lethargic.
  • Ascites: fluid builds up in the abdomen when the heart cannot keep up with rapid growth.
  • Sudden death syndrome: acute heart failure. Brief but intense, so it adds almost no hours.

The project publishes a lower and upper bound for each cause as well; they are not yet loaded here (see the repository's TODO).

The same hours by intensity, with uncertainty

The project ran its estimate many times with every input varied over its plausible range and reports the mean and a 90% interval (the 5th to 95th percentile of those runs; no median or 95% interval is published). Your meal's share of each, mean then interval in brackets:

Most of the uncertainty is here, in the pain estimates, not in the weights: the intervals are roughly ±40% around the mean. The by-cause figures rest on the same evidence, so treat them as carrying about the same uncertainty. The two breakdowns come from different releases of the same data and differ by about 4%, so read the by-cause numbers as proportions rather than exact hours. The bracket on the total adds the individual intervals, which slightly overstates its width.

What the intensities mean (project definitions):

  • Annoying: unpleasant, but does not disrupt normal activity.
  • Hurtful: hard to ignore; the bird is aware of it most of the time.
  • Disabling: continuously distressing; no enjoyment is possible.
  • Excruciating: intolerable even for seconds.
Sources and what is left out
  1. Live weight (P, N): USDA NASS, Poultry Production and Value 2024 Summary (9.33 billion broilers, 61.1 billion lb).
  2. Dressing d = 0.75: University of Maine Extension, Understanding Poultry Yields.
  3. Edible share e = 0.74: University of Wisconsin Extension, Bird Breakdown.
  4. Cooking yield y = 0.75: USDA Table of Cooking Yields for Meat and Poultry.
  5. Lifetime T = 47 days: National Chicken Council, U.S. Broiler Performance.
  6. Hours of pain by intensity, mean and 90% interval: Welfare Footprint Institute, Broilers, conventional scenario. Consistent with their finding that the Better Chicken Commitment prevents 33 [13 to 53] h of Disabling, 79 [-99 to 260] h of Hurtful and 25 [5 to 45] s of Excruciating pain per bird.
  7. Hours of pain by cause: Welfare Footprint Institute, Pain-Track for broilers, "time in pain per harm" for the average bird.

Not included: pain during transport and slaughter, breeder birds, and diseases the project did not model. The numbers are conservative.
